Alainpaul Spring 2027 Ready-to-Wear Collection

Alainpaul Spring 2027 Ready-to-Wear collection, runway looks, beauty, models, and reviews.

By Tina Isaac-Goizé

October 1, 2026

Last season Alain Paul focused on light. “But what happens when a dancer exits the stage?” the designer mused before his spring show. The tiny flashlights handed out at the door gave show guests a hint: it wasn’t noon yet, but we were headed to a rave.

So, how might dancers dress for a rave? Just as ravers dance: haphazardly, depending on whatever spirit moves you. It’s community that’s the point.

“We’re in a time now where it’s hard to speak about the world,” Paul said. “I wanted to speak about dance as this kind of universal language—we can all communicate with it, and right now being together is so important.”

The opening look suggested that a dancer might grab her tights, wrap them into a bustier and throw on stirrup pants, here in tailored stretch wool anchored by the brand’s new pointy-toe pumps. A shrunken wraparound sweater was paired with low-slung leather pants (knee pad accessories optional) or a leather pencil skirt with utility pockets. Elsewhere, a leather sash, here worn as a bandeau, had a zip for stashing just the basics, because why cart a bag to a rave?

Some of the most striking pieces were those that could be spotted in the dark: a diaphanous silver bustier in silk organza woven with metal thread looked like material grabbed from the costume department and thrown over easy, silver-foiled, distressed denims. A quicksilver lamé top was tied with a bow at the neck and left open; a sylver nylon dress was tunneled with drawstrings. Paul explained that he wanted to treat nylon like silk and vice versa: several numbers were likewise run through with cords and stoppers to let the wearer adjust as occasion demands. In the same spirit a tailored jacket with a reverse-peak lapel riffed on an Alainpaul signature, adjusting with elastic and three buttons in back.

Sportswear-inspired pieces in wet-finish technical fabric with period-piece balloon sleeves also looked new. Likewise plundered from wardrobe came jacquard panniers, sashes and tutus, now worked into “thousand-ruffle” panties, skirts or gowns. Those, like the hand knits, are time intensive (and heavy) so are available only by special order.

In addition to staging his show to a live dance performance developed with the choreographer Eric Christison, the sound designer Senjan Jansen and Shygirl, Paul paid tribute to Pina Bausch: a portrait of the late dance legend appeared on a gray tank worn like a plastron over a tailored jacket. He also borrowed her words for a message tee: “Dance, dance, otherwise we are lost.”

“Usually you’d have Joy Division or something on a shirt like that,” Paul offered. “But I think she was quite rock ‘n’ roll.” So, too, were many of the looks here.
