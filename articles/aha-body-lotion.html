Do You Actually Need an AHA Body Lotion?

The best AHA body lotions use glycolic and lactic acids to resurface skin and address issues like keratosis pilaris and rough skin. Shop our top picks.

By Deanna Pai

September 30, 2026

Exfoliation for the body isn’t exactly new (see: body scrubs), but alpha hydroxy acids (AHAs) take the concept to the next level. AHA body lotions pair these chemical exfoliants with rich, buttery bases to gently resurface skin and replenish moisture in a single step, streamlining your routine while smoothing and softening skin.

If you’re content with your current body lotion, that’s great. In fact, “not everyone needs an AHA body lotion,” says Tiffany Libby, MD, a board-certified dermatologist in Providence. However, she says they can be especially helpful if you have rough or bumpy texture like keratosis pilaris, hyperpigmentation or dullness, crepiness, or fine lines. “AHAs work by loosening the bonds between dead skin cells, helping them shed more efficiently, so the skin feels smoother and looks more even,” says Dr. Libby.

While there are a variety of AHAs out there, your best bets for the body tend to be lactic and glycolic acids. “Lactic acid is a little gentler and has humectant properties, so it’s great for dry or sensitive skin, while glycolic acid penetrates more effectively and can be especially helpful for rough texture, KP, hyperpigmentation, and fine lines,” says Mamina Turegano, MD, a board-certified dermatologist in New Orleans.

You can also find these AHAs paired with other smoothing ingredients, like urea and beta hydroxy acids, to amp up their benefits—along with barrier-friendly actives that help keep your skin comfortable and calm alongside that exfoliation. With that in mind, scroll ahead for our favorites.

Everything You Need to Know

Which AHA is the most effective for the body

Alpha hydroxy acids are actually a group of acids, like mandelic, glycolic, and lactic acids. But when it comes to treating the skin below your neck, there are two that stand out: glycolic and lactic. "Glycolic acid has a relatively small molecular size and is a great choice when the goal is more noticeable exfoliation for rough texture or discoloration," says Dr. Libby. Lactic acid, on the other hand, is a larger molecule, so it sticks near the skin’s surface and therefore tends to be gentler. "It also has humectant properties, so I particularly like it for someone who is dry or more sensitive but still wants that smoothing effect," she says.

How to Use an AHA Body Lotion

If you’re ready to add an AHA body lotion to your routine, begin by using it two to three times a week, and then bump it up to daily use if your skin can tolerate that, according to Dr. Turegano. The strength matters here too—the higher the strength, the more gradual your use should be. “Because AHAs are exfoliating, going too hard with a high strength too quickly can cause irritation and actually make dryness or discoloration worse,” she says.

As important as the frequency is when you apply it: Don’t do it immediately after shaving or waxing, for starters, or if your skin is irritated, broken, or compromised in any way. (For example, if you have an eczema flare, that’s not the time to start using one.) And hold off if you notice any changes in your skin. “If you're seeing persistent redness, burning, peeling, or increased sensitivity, you're probably overdoing it,” says Dr. Libby.

And finally, make sure you’re also applying body sunscreen regularly. “AHAs can also increase sun sensitivity, so sun protection matters on exposed areas, especially the arms, chest, and legs,” Dr. Libby says. AHA body lotions might be more high-maintenance than your average body cream, but the smoother, softer skin they offer might just be worth the extra effort.
