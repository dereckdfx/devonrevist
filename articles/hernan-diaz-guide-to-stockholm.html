Hernan Diaz Knows Where to Get the Best Cod in Stockholm

Diaz's latest, 'Ply,' comes out today. Here he offers his guide to Stockholm, the city in which he spent much of his childhood.

By Riza Cruz

September 29, 2026

Born in Buenos Aires, Hernan Diaz moved to Stockholm at age two after the 1976 military coup, only returning when democracy was reestablished seven years later. “All my childhood memories are in Swedish, a language that I still speak fluently,” he says. “Those sunlit summer midnights and pitch-black winter mornings shaped me in decisive ways. I also read my first books in Swedish, which has made me nostalgic for everything related to Astrid Lindgren. My parents owned a beautiful country house where, supposedly, the Beatles found refuge during their Swedish tour—I’m not sure this is true, but have no intention of debunking the family myth.”

The author’s Pulitzer Prize–winning novel Trust is being adapted as a Netflix series starring and executive-produced by Kate Winslet. He’s also an executive producer, along with writer/director Todd Haynes. His first novel, In the Distance, was a finalist for the Pulitzer, and his latest, Ply (Riverheard), set in the future, comes out today. “Swedish was my first literary language as a child, and why I ended up somewhat spontaneously acquiring English in my teens,” Diaz says. “Being surrounded by the beautiful music of Scandinavian vowels and the transparency of its syntax is always a source of immense joy.”

He keeps up to date with Sweden’s literary scene, has friends and family there, and tries to visit Stockholm every year. “Despite having spent most of my life in New York,” he says, “I consider myself to be—in limited but decisive ways—part Swedish.”

Below, his recs for his adopted city. May they bring you immense joy.

Best time to visit, weather-wise: Summers are famously glorious. The light. That watery blue sky… Most European countries shut down in August, but Swedes tend to take July off, which makes this my favorite month to visit. Everyone is just so happy and relaxed.

Favorite hotel: For the last few years, I’ve consistently stayed at Rival, owned by ABBA’s own Benny Andersson. It’s right on Mariatorget, a lovely square surrounded by excellent coffee shops, restaurants, a natural wine bar, and bookshops. Make sure to get a room with a balcony for private hipster-gazing with a cocktail. But Rival now has a…rival. The rooms at Stockholm’s Stadshotell (formerly a women’s shelter) are exquisite, lovely examples of soothing Scandinavian minimalism. The restaurant is fabulous.

Best restaurant: Nothing beats Prinsen for old-school charm. Very Swedish menu—herring, prawns, biff Rydberg. During my last trip, I looked for the best cod in town. They have it. I had dinner there so many times that one of the servers gave me the beautiful enamel pin they wear on their chic uniforms.

Best dish: I love having lunch at Bakfickan, the restaurant attached to the Royal Swedish Opera. The main room is beautiful, but I prefer the bar. I have discovered that their meatballs, the best in town, pair really well with Chablis. Oh, and summer potatoes. Summer potatoes are very, very important.

To see art: Fotografiska and Moderna Museet.

To buy flowers: Christoffers Blommor, in the old town.

Bookstore or library: Rönnel’s Antikvariat is one of the most beautiful, surprising, and coolest bookstores in the world. So many unexpected gems. They also have gorgeous prints. And the space itself is unbelievable. Nord Books is an exquisitely curated English-language bookshop steps away from Mariatorget. Within walking distance is Söderbokhandeln, with a great selection of books on art and music, many of them in English.

Nature escape: The whole city is, of course, an archipelago. The center is relatively small, but the whole archipelago, known as Skärgården, comprises over 30,000 islands. That means that you are always a short boat trip away from a bucolic destination. Some of the ferries cost as much as a subway trip—and run almost as frequently.

For peace and quiet: Stockholm has one of the most beautiful parks in the world. You can easily spend a whole day in Djurgården. Should that be the case, lunch at Rosendals Trädgård is an unforgettable experience. They grow all their food organically right there, in the park, by the restaurant. Bonkers good.

Best way to get around town: By foot. If you are spending most of your time in central Stockholm, you are pretty much an hour away from everywhere. I love the subway system too.

Best view: Like so many European cities’, Stockholm’s skyline is quite low. This is why Himlen (which means “heaven” or “sky”), a rooftop bar on the 25th floor right on the water, offers unparalleled views. Immediately underneath, and with equally breathtaking views, is Gondolen. Amazing fish and a fabulous wine list. If the weather is nice, dine alfresco by the water, facing the skyline, at Måsen.

Best place for people-watching: I grew up in Stockholm during the heyday of social democracy. This is why I am always surprised to see Italian sports cars driven by overly tan men with watches the size of Swedish cinnamon buns. To witness this scene, nothing is better than a sidewalk seat and a cocktail at Café Riche, in the middle of the posh neighborhood of Östermalm.

Best place for wine: Matbaren probably has the best wine list in Stockholm. It’s right next to the famed Grand Hotel, and the small dishes are immensely creative and elegant. Babette has a lovely natural wine selection. Counterintuitive as it may seem to do this in Stockholm, do order the stracciatella pizza. Café Nizza is a charming spot in Södermalm with impeccably unfussy dishes and fun bottles. Savant is where you want to go if you just want a well-curated glass and a nibble.
