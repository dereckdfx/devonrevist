Niccolò Pasqualetti Spring 2027 Ready-to-Wear Collection

Niccolò Pasqualetti Spring 2027 Ready-to-Wear collection, runway looks, beauty, models, and reviews.

By Tiziana Cardini

October 4, 2026

For Niccolò Pasqualetti, clothes begin as ideas about form. Not fashion form, exactly, but the kind that belongs to sculpture: a square, a triangle, a flat surface waiting to be transformed. This season, the Tuscan designer looked to the Brazilian artist Lygia Clark, whose participatory works invited viewers to become part of the artwork itself. Pasqualetti translated that notion into garments that “only fully exist once they encounter a body,” they said.

It sounds cerebral, and it was. Yet Pasqualetti has steadily developed a language that makes intellectual propositions feel rather natural. Geometric cuts, asymmetries, layered constructions, and a gender fluid approach to dressing have become his signature vocabulary, giving shape to clothes that are sensual, slightly strange, and always a little askew.

Many pieces began as flat forms, squares, rectangles, triangles, that shifted once worn. Cotton poplin was drawn into volume with cords, skirts unfolded in angular layers, and column dresses were sliced with incisions that fractured their surface. Fabric combinations added another level of complexity: gauze met suede, mesh sat beneath gabardine, silk was laminated to resemble crumpled silver foil. Throughout, Pasqualetti pursued a productive tension between precision and softness, construction and instinct.

They were at his strongest when geometric clarity met his off-kilter sensibility, while some of the more emphatic sculptural gestures—bulbous protrusions that seemed to be molded and crumpled around dresses and skirts—were less persuasive. Still, that willingness to experiment is part of Pasqualetti’s appeal, and of his continuing exploration of the space between abstraction and wearability.

A collection of crystal-inspired fragrances, Andara, was launched recently, another addition to the world they building piece by piece. Like the perfumes, their clothes suggest that identity is never fixed, but continually shaped through encounters, perceptions, and instinct. A triangle can become a dress, and a dress can become something more elusive: an object of movement, sensation, and expression.
