Christopher Esber Spring 2027 Ready-to-Wear Collection

Christopher Esber Spring 2027 Ready-to-Wear collection, runway looks, beauty, models, and reviews.

By Tina Isaac-Goizé

October 6, 2026

When you consider the quiet charge of Christopher Esber’s work, pretty much the last pop icon that might spring to mind is Elvis Presley. Esber is more about a well-placed cutout than swagger.

For spring, however, the designer was drawn to the King’s tailoring—in the early years, not the Vegas era. “I was looking at a lot of suits Elvis was wearing in the ’50s, lots of studded belts,” he said backstage before the show. “I was just loving the elongated proportions.”

You could see it in the flared trousers and the jackets with long torsos and high pockets. The designer also tracked down a trove of ’50s studs and used those on belts that, rather than stick to the waist, migrated onto jackets, where they helped to shape the tailoring. The neckerchief, too, came back in new iterations—draped on an asymmetrical gilet held in place by an emerald-colored brooch, teased into sculptural peaks on bustiers, and fashioned into a metal choker worn with geometric white, ceramic-coated lace. “I like this idea of taking things that are ornamental but making them architectural,” the designer offered.

That sense of precision looked great on leather, too. A sundress that, from a distance, looked like deconstructed white cotton eyelet turned out to be perforated leather; a black version was just as sleek. A short, cropped, latticed number looked like a winner too.

Esber’s clients love his particular brand of minimalism: his draped jersey dresses, worked with semi-precious beads, wood or metal, were plentiful in the audience. This season, the idea returned with an imposing stack of tiger’s eyes. The runway also offered up some strong tailored jackets and A-line skirts slit high on the thigh.

But Esber also set off down another, more conceptual, path with an assist from the artist Anna Stüdeli. Esber called her collages “very beautiful but also strange,” adding, “I feel like this is kind of the balance I’m always trying to strike with every collection.” They turned up in glossy bandeaux, low-slung skirts, a shift and a trench made of satin-backed PVC. A couple of numbers recomposed the artist’s work through clear resin cabochons, with motifs printed on the undersides and the pieces reassembled like a jigsaw puzzle. From the right angle you could see an eye and red fingernails on one garment; another seemed to present a sunset scene. If this lineup is any indication, it looks like Esber’s quiet charge is about to get quite a bit louder.
