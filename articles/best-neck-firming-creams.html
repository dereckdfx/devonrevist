9 Neck Creams That Actually Smooth Fine Lines and Improve Crepey Texture

As important as any face moisturizer, the best neck cream is essential in a skin-care routine. Shop derm-approved picks from SkinCeuticals, Tatcha, and more.

By Jenny Berg

October 14, 2026

Nora Ephron made the sentiment iconic, but she certainly wasn’t alone. At 46, I, too, feel bad about my neck. (Live footage of myself from the chin up on Teams doesn’t help.) Therefore, it’s no surprise that the best neck creams are at the top of many a shopper’s list—and not only for those of us with mature skin.

“The neck has become a frequent topic of conversation in our practice, particularly among younger patients who are beginning to notice signs of ‘tech neck,’ or the visible creases that can develop from repeatedly looking down at a phone,” says Emily Lebowitz Luks, MD, a board-certified dermatologist based in New York. “The rise of remote work and virtual meetings has brought heightened attention to an area that is often overlooked in skin-care routines, despite being one of the first places to show signs of aging.”

Dr. Lebowitz Luks says fine lines, crepey texture, and skin laxity are the most common concerns for patients booking in-office neck treatments such as radiofrequency skin tightening and microneedling—but these treatments don’t act alone. “They are often complemented by topical skin-care regimens to help maintain results,” she notes. “Rather than waiting for visible signs of aging, many younger patients are embracing the concept of ‘prejuvenation’ by proactively incorporating targeted skin care into their routines.”

According to Noah Gratch, MD, a board-certified dermatologist in New York and Long Island, prejuvenation starts with sunscreen. “Applying a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher to the neck every single morning is the most important step in slowing collagen breakdown,” he says. With that baseline established, layering on neck creams with increasingly sophisticated active ingredients is the new norm in neck care.

“Using a neck cream can help maintain the skin’s suppleness and complement professional treatments,” Dr. Gratch confirms—but he stresses that routine use is key: “I often tell my patients that a high-quality cream on the neck is not about overnight results but about consistency and supporting the skin long-term.” According to the pros, the nine formulas ahead are the true standouts in the neck-care category.

Everything You Need to Know

What is a neck cream?

“Neck cream is a moisturizer that is formulated to address skin concerns of the neck. These are usually crepiness, sagging, lines—including ‘tech neck’ lines—and wrinkles,” says Engelman.

What causes neck wrinkles?

The neck is notoriously quick to show signs of aging, and biology bears at least some of the blame. “Neck skin is thinner than facial skin and remains exposed to the same environmental stressors, like UV light and pollution,” says Libby. “Neck skin also has a lower oil gland composition than facial skin, and thus produces less of the necessary oils to keep the skin barrier well moisturized.”

Plus, the latest gadgets don’t help the quest for crease-free necks. “This generational focus on constant tech use has increased downward motions [of the neck],” Libby says. “These downward motions physically cause creasing, which is not doing any favors for an area of the body that has thinner skin to begin with.”

Why does the skin around the neck and décolletage age more rapidly?

Not only is neck skin prone to aging in general—it’s likely to sag and wrinkle fast. “Because there is naturally less collagen in the neck due to the thinness of the dermis in that area, the neck is particularly prone to premature aging,” Engelman says.

“This is also an area that is highly exposed to the sun and the elements, which contributes to more rapid aging.” Then, of course, there’s human error. “Oftentimes, the neck is a place that people forget or neglect when applying serums, moisturizers, or sunscreen,” says Engelman. “I always remind my patients that skin doesn’t just stop below the jawline.” Lucky, a well-formulated neck cream can swoop in to save the area from neglect, and offer a range of benefits beyond that.

Do neck creams actually work?

According to Kristina Collins, MD, FAAD, a board-certified dermatologist and Mohs micrographic surgeon in Austin, neck creams do work—assuming you swipe them on with realistic expectations. “Neck creams can improve hydration, skin texture, and the appearance of fine lines, but they won't produce face-lift-level results,” she says. However, she says that consistent use of a well-formulated neck cream can make a visible difference over time, particularly when it’s combined with sun protection.

What to Look for in a Neck Cream

“The skin on the neck is thinner and more prone to sagging due to fewer oil glands and frequent movement, but targeted ingredients can help maintain firmness and elasticity,” Dr. Collins says. When choosing a neck cream, she specifically recommends looking for ingredients—including those listed below—that support collagen production, improve hydration, and protect against environmental damage.

  • Peptides: “These amino acid chains signal the skin to produce more collagen and elastin, helping to improve firmness and reduce fine lines over time,” Dr. Collins says.
  • Retinol: Collins calls this vitamin A derivative “a gold-standard ingredient for boosting cell turnover and stimulating collagen production,” and notes that it helps smooth wrinkles and improve skin texture.
  • Ceramides: “These lipid molecules strengthen the skin barrier, preventing moisture loss and keeping the delicate neck area hydrated and resilient,” Collins says.
  • Hyaluronic Acid: Dr. Collins says that this humectant, which helps the skin attract and retain moisture, plumps up the skin on the neck to reduce crepiness.
  • Niacinamide: A form of vitamin B3, niacinamide helps improve skin elasticity, reduce redness, and support an even skin tone, Dr. Collins says.
  • Coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10): Dr. Collins recommends CoQ10 to fortify the delicate neck skin with antioxidant protection. “The ingredient helps defend against environmental stressors while supporting collagen production,” she says. The result? “Firmer, more youthful-looking skin.”
  • Vitamin C: Another powerful antioxidant, vitamin C protects the skin from free radicals and UV light, helps fade dark spots, and can be instrumental in skin tightening. “I have used vitamin C in my clinic for years to help with elasticity and tighten the skin around the neck and décolletage,” celebrity esthetician Kate Somerville previously told Devon .

The Best Way to Apply Neck Cream

Neck creams can be used day and night, with an emphasis on SPF-infused creams in the morning. “Apply the cream in a gentle upward motion to not tug downward on the skin,” says Libby. “As mentioned, the skin of the neck is very thin so it’s important to treat this area with care,” Engelman cautions. “Tugging or pulling on the skin can exacerbate its lines and crepiness.”

What are the best ways to prevent neck wrinkles?

Preventing neck wrinkles is a holistic affair. Below, Dr. Collins breaks down her top tips—from the best skin-care ingredients to use to the best posture to adopt—for keeping the neck smooth and supple.

  • Daily Sunscreen: “UV exposure accelerates collagen breakdown and skin laxity,” Dr. Collins says. “Apply a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher on the neck every morning, even if you’re indoors near windows.”
  • Moisturize: “Hydrated skin is more resilient,” she says. “Look for moisturizers with hyaluronic acid, ceramides, or glycerin to plump and protect the skin barrier.”
  • Topical Antioxidants: Dr. Collins recommends using vitamin C and other antioxidants to help neutralize free radicals and prevent wrinkles caused by sun-induced damage.
  • Retinoids/Retinol: “Regular use of retinoids boosts collagen production and improves skin texture,” Dr. Collins says. “Start with a low-strength retinol to avoid irritation, and apply gradually.”
  • Mind Your Posture: Constantly looking down at phones or laptops can deepen horizontal lines, Dr. Collins says. “Position devices at eye level whenever possible.”
  • Healthy Lifestyle Habits: To support skin health from within, Dr. Collins says to avoid smoking, get adequate sleep, and stick to a balanced diet that’s rich in antioxidants and omega-3s.

What do dermatologists recommend for neck wrinkles?

To treat active neck wrinkles, Dr. Collins suggests “neck-specific skin care,” such as the creams listed in this edit, and prescription retinoids like tretinoin: “They are more potent than over-the-counter retinol options and can significantly improve fine lines and texture.” Certain professional treatments, such as those listed below, can also help diminish the appearance of neck wrinkles.

  • Professional Chemical Peels: “Mild peels with glycolic or lactic acid help exfoliate and stimulate new collagen over time,” Dr. Collins says.
  • Laser Treatments: Fractional or non-ablative lasers resurface the skin, which promotes collagen remodeling for smoother, firmer skin, she says.
  • Radiofrequency and Ultrasound Devices: “Noninvasive procedures like Ultherapy or RF microneedling can tighten and lift the neck by stimulating deep collagen production,” Dr. Collins shares.
  • Injectables: Neuromodulators like Botox can soften dynamic bands, Dr. Collins says, while hyaluronic acid fillers can improve volume and smooth creases.
  • Collagen-Stimulating Treatments: As another option, Dr. Collins notes, biostimulatory injectables like Sculptra can gradually boost collagen for long-term improvement.
