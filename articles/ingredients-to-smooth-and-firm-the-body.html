Found: The Best Ingredients to Firm and Smooth the Body

Slather on the best ingredients to firm and smooth the body, which help spur cell turnover and aid with crepiness and uneven texture. Shop our top picks.

By Deanna Pai

September 28, 2026

If you’re seeing roughness, crepiness, or otherwise textured skin below the neck, you’re in luck: The best ingredients to firm and smooth the body can make a noticeable difference. While much of the attention has long been focused on the face, more and more brands are now applying time-tested actives, like retinoids and alpha hydroxy acids, to body-care formulas. These days, retinol body lotions abound, as do body serums that aim to resurface and renew skin on the arms and legs. And they all share the common goal of delivering and maintaining supple, soft skin from head to toe. In fact, “you can often combine treatment and moisturization in the same step,” says board-certified dermatologist Sarah Sung, MD. “Ingredients such as lactic acid and urea are great examples because they can help loosen rough, built-up skin while also improving hydration.”

That’s noteworthy, since skin on the body is very different from skin on the face. “The face generally has a higher concentration of sebaceous glands, while areas such as the arms and legs tend to produce less oil and can therefore be more prone to dryness,” says board-certified dermatologist Anetta Reszko, MD. “Furthermore, neck, chest, skin folds, and recently shaved areas can actually be quite sensitive.” That drier, possibly more sensitive skin means that a moisturizing base isn’t optional.

Keep in mind that topical treatments can only do so much, especially if you’re seeing sagging or very lax skin. “Creams improve texture and mild crepiness, but they won’t lift truly loose skin,” says board-certified dermatologist Michael Tassavor, MD. “And none of it works without daily sunscreen on exposed areas like the chest, arms, and hands.” Only an in-office procedure can truly lift or tighten skin. But even so, topical creams and lotions may still merit a spot on your shelf, even if you see your dermatologist regularly. “You can do all the in-office treatments you want, but if you ignore what you do to your skin every day, you can undermine those results,” says Sung. All to say: Skin care still matters—there’s just a limit on what it can do alone, she adds. With that in mind, scroll ahead for the best ingredients to firm and smooth the body.

Retinoids

Retinoids are hard to beat when it comes to firming skin—both for your face and your body. They effectively “tell your skin cells to behave younger,” says Tassavor. “They switch on collagen production and slow down the enzymes that break collagen down.” That allows them to target a host of issues, from signs of sun damage to changes in skin texture and tone.

Because of that, “patients who notice rough texture, sun damage, fine lines, or early crepiness on areas such as the arms, shoulders, chest, back, thighs, or buttocks can benefit from retinol,” says Reszko. As with retinoids for your face, start by using a new retinol body product two to three times a week, then add more days if your skin doesn’t seem especially dry or red.

Exfoliating Acids

For rough skin texture, it’s hard to do better than regular exfoliation. Chemical exfoliants are especially powerful—namely, alpha hydroxy acids (AHAs) like glycolic and lactic acids. “AHAs work by loosening the bonds between dead skin cells at the surface, allowing them to shed more efficiently and revealing smoother, brighter-looking skin,” says Reszko.

With steady use, glycolic acid can even support new collagen too, according to Tassavor. For that reason, “it’s ideal if your main complaint is rough, bumpy, or crepey skin,” he says, warning that it does make skin more sun sensitive, so proper sun protection is a must. Lactic acid, for its part, is gentler than glycolic acid. Moreover, “lactic acid has the added benefit of being a humectant, so it tends to be a nice choice for someone who is both rough and dry,” says Sung. Whichever acid you choose, though, they tend to be a good option if you have superficial bumps, like keratosis pilaris, or all-around roughness.

Urea

Urea might just be the unsung hero of body smoothing. But it deserves more time in the spotlight, mostly because “it does two jobs very well,” says Sung. “At lower concentrations, it draws water into the skin and improves hydration; at higher concentrations, it also helps soften skin and break down thick, rough keratin.” That makes it ideal for very dry, flaky, or scaly areas of the body, like around the elbows and knees, though you can use it all over.

Barrier Support

No body-care routine is complete without moisturizing ingredients—in particular, those that support the skin barrier. “Skin generally looks smoother and plumper when the barrier is healthy and adequately hydrated,” says Sung. “A moisturizer can make crepey skin look noticeably smoother because hydrated skin is plumper.” The effect is more fleeting than what you might see with brand-new collagen production, but it still makes a difference on a daily basis—not just in how your skin looks, but in terms of comfort as well.

One ingredient to look for is a ceramide, a lipid naturally found in the skin barrier. “Adding ceramides back through a moisturizer can help reduce water loss and support a healthier barrier, which makes skin feel softer and look smoother and less crepey,” says Sung. “They won’t stimulate collagen in the way a retinoid can, but they make almost every active in your body-care routine easier to tolerate.” Whatever other ingredients you choose to add, consider the lineup incomplete without a lipid-rich moisturizer.
