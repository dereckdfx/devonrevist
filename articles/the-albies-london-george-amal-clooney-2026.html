At The Albies, Amal and George Clooney Make Sure the Real-Life Heroes Take Center Stage

As one would imagine, Amal and George Clooney certainly have no issue pulling a crowd. As such, the host table placement at their fifth annual foundation gala, The Albies, did not disappoint.

By Christie Grimm

October 9, 2026

As one would imagine, Amal and George Clooney certainly have no issue pulling a crowd. As such, the host table placement at their fifth annual foundation gala, The Albies, did not disappoint.

On George’s left sat his longtime co-stars and friends Laura Dern and Brad Pitt. On the actor’s right, rapper and activist Vic Mensa and Downton Abbey’s Lord Grantham himself, Hugh Bonneville. With the inimitable couple separated only by the table’s elaborate edible display of Italian delicacies, Amal, draped in a gilded off-the-shoulder sequin gown by Gucci, was shoulder-to-shoulder with Devon’s Anna Wintour, musician Jackson Browne, and actress Emma Thompson.

Still, even in a room bursting with headline-worthy names, the Clooneys ensured the spotlight shone brightest on the evening’s honorees: Kyrgyz journalists Bolot Temirov and Makhabat Tazhibek Kyzy, Malawian girls’ rights activist Vanessa Mwangala, American photojournalist Lynsey Addario, Congolese NGO Justice Plus, and Microsoft Vice Chair and President Brad Smith.

When it came to choosing this year’s award recipients, George remarked, “You have to look at things that are going on in the news. We’re looking for people that are shining a light on [an issue], can help put a protective shield over someone, or prevent them from being thrown in jail or killed.”

Journalist Christiane Amanpour, who stepped out for the occasion in a red jacquard pantsuit by Derek Lam, echoed Clooney’s focus on providing protection for those relentlessly working to hold corrupt systems accountable. “Right now is the time to put a focus on human rights around the world. We need more big names and big organizations who can say ‘Hang on, this is important, this space has to be defended’—especially while everybody else is cutting back, George and Amal lend their name and their heft and public profile to this. It’s super important.”
