Reformation X Alexa Chung Is Coming: Here’s Everything You Need To Know

Alexa Chung’s first Reformation collaboration has arrived—and Devon readers can shop the 30-piece collection a full day before anyone else.

By Joy Montgomery

October 4, 2026

In our social media-obsessed world, It-girls have become as ubiquitous as iPhones; it seems as though every day there’s another multi-hyphenate twenty-something making a bid for supremacy. In many ways, Alexa Chung can be counted among the final cohort of It-girls who forged their fame beyond the confines of the social feed. From TV presenter to front-row darling, brand founder to author, Chung knows a thing or two about reinvention, yet the cultural relevance she has maintained over the last two decades has always felt true to the wry-humored fashion plate who first got her big break on Channel 4’s Popworld back in 2006.

While Chung’s career portfolio has been ever-evolving, her distinctive personal style has remained a constant—not to mention a source of inspiration for a generation of fashion girlies who came of age in the 2010s. And it’s this clear aesthetic vision that is palpable in Chung’s first collaboration with L.A.-based fashion brand Reformation, which drops tomorrow.

“I always start with my own wardrobe first; what I feel on a personal level that I’m missing or dreaming about,” Chung says of building the collection. “Vintage shopping, which has always been a pleasure for me, is also inspiring. I went to Rockstar Boudoir in Crouch End and found this beautiful 1930s velvet coat. I love the sort of elegant eveningwear that feels slinky and luxurious, but you can still style with denim. I think this set the tone. If this collection had a time, it’s 9 pm, you’re with the gals, and it’s cocktail o’clock.”

In many ways, the 30-piece collection is a “This Is Your Life” of Chung’s fashion archive. There’s a double-breasted camel coat that recalls a coat she wore to London Fashion Week in 2012; a pair of knee-high, block-heeled boots, reminiscent of a pair she wore to a Prada party in 2020; a lace-trimmed cocktail dress not dissimilar to a butter-coloured slip she styled under a wax jacket to Glastonbury in 2024. And then there are the logo tees and high-rise, straight-leg jeans, the sort of unassuming yet inexplicably cool basics that have become Chung’s sartorial calling card.

x Alexa Chung Patti leather jacket

x Alexa Chung Patti leather slip skirt

“It’s handy that I’ve always loved classic things, and it’s not like I’ve been ultra experimental with trendy items,” Chung says of building a wardrobe that has stood the test of time. “There’ll be anomalies in there, but ultimately, I love men’s shirts, classic 501s, navy-blue jumpers, ballet flats, a sensible bag. All the things I’ve always adored, and still do, are timeless, which, I guess, is what makes them timely.”

The collection is sure to sate the appetite of Chung’s millennial superfans; however, the resurgence of the indie-sleaze aesthetic – which has seen a whole new generation tap into the biker jacket and band tee vibe that Chung became synonymous with in the 2010s – will no doubt draw in a younger crowd. What does she make of the comeback? “It brings such a smile to my face, because for a while there, I thought that it had all died out and no one was going to plug the amp in ever again. Although people don’t really use it as a tribe signifier anymore in the same way that we did. I don’t think it’s a full faithful rendition of the ‘Hedi [Slimane] boy’.”

x Alexa Chung Legacy gown

x Alexa Chung Magic velvet bag

Despite her successes, Chung admits that she’s apprehensive about the collection dropping: “I really hope people like it. I’ve done collaborations before, but they’ve been in quite specific categories, and this has the opportunity to reach more people,” she says. “The thing I love about Reformation is that it sells everything, which is really fun, but also makes it more similar to my own brand. And I think that’s why I’m nervous – I’m hoping that I’m catering to everyone, not just myself.” Something tells us it will be a surefire hit.
