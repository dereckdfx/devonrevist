Noir Kei Ninomiya Spring 2027 Ready-to-Wear Collection

Noir Kei Ninomiya Spring 2027 Ready-to-Wear collection, runway looks, beauty, models, and reviews.

By Luke Leitch

October 3, 2026

Kei Ninomiya opened with what looked a little like two court dresses wrenched inside out and turned upside down. Rigid translucent ribs rose and flared around the body like inverted panniers, from which long strings of pearls fell almost to the floor. The flowers placed in the mouth of the second model pointed to Ninomiya’s stated theme: petal rebellion.

Many fashion collections have been arranged around flowers, but none of them looked like this. In Ninomiya’s hands, gauze moves in mysterious ways: Here it was shaped into colored cloudlike growths that bloomed up the body. Two models were clad in coned black coils, into which were placed long-stemmed lilies. Another wore a miniskirt of mesh from which beaded flowers were projected on springy wire stalks. Her body was pressed with red dried flowers that shed as she walked.

Structures that could have represented wings as well as petals flew from the shoulders of two bikers. A long black dress was made from a riveted network of flower-shaped cutouts. Backstage, Ninomiya, always reticent, did let slip the phrase “armor of peace.”

Around these flowers seemed to gather an antagonistic force that was hinted at in the opening duo of dresses. Most models wore ringleted wigs that bounced fetchingly with movement and resembled judicial wigs. There was a side mission into playing-card imagery, with all its language of hierarchy, opposition, and fixed order: A pair of dresses was built from cards, and other looks saw Ninomiya bend his trademark exoskeletal sculpture into parts conjured from the molded outlines of the four suits.

The final two looks resembled the extravagant bouquets fashion designers once customarily sent reviewers along with thank-you notes but these days rarely bother with. Transformed into clothes, these bouquets placed the models inside crinkled, rubbery sheaths from which packed blooms pushed upward and downward around the bodies within. This was a darkly beautiful and sometimes disturbing collection from Ninomiya: Noir to a T.
