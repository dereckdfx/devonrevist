24 White-Tie Wedding Guest Dresses Fit for the Most Formal Occasions

Finding the right white-tie wedding guest dress for an ultra-formal ceremony comes with its own requirements; here, a bridal stylist breaks down the dress code.

By Corinne Pierre-Louis

September 30, 2026

The strictest dress code in Western formalwear, white-tie attire sits a rung above black tie and has barely changed since its 19th-century origins, when society balls and formal dinners called for tailcoats on men and full-length ball gowns on women. Today, it’s largely reserved for occasions like the Nobel Prize banquet and the occasional royal event—not exactly the sort of directive most wedding guests encounter often, if ever. So if an invitation bearing those two words has landed in your mailbox, don’t panic. The guidelines are few; they’re just firm.

Devon’s Guide to White-Tie Wedding Guest Dresses:

Chief among them: Your dress must reach the floor, which means midi and tea-length styles are out, as are sleek jumpsuits and coordinated separates. Fabric also matters: Duchesse satin, silk faille, heavy crepe, brocade, satin, and velvet have the weight and shine to suit the occasion.

Color, on the other hand, is where you can have a bit more fun. White tie is strict with silhouette but generous with palette, and fall and winter weddings lend themselves especially well to rich, saturated shades in red, violet, emerald, gold, and blue. Try a scarlet A-line from Carolina Herrera or an emerald gown from Bernadette for sculptural silhouettes. A black dress is always appropriate, too—Taller Marmo’s Notte maxi, with its striking combination of feathers and sequins, makes the case for full-on glamour. Just skip anything neon (it never reads as formal), and, of course, leave white and ivory to the bride.

As for accessories, let one piece of jewelry take the lead here, whether it’s a crystal-encrusted bracelet or a family heirloom ring. Opera-length gloves are an appropriate finishing touch—they’re no longer required, but why not embrace this rare opportunity for high-drama dressing? And let’s not forget outerwear: Instead of your everyday jacket, reach for an evening wrap or a tailored coat that matches the formality of your look if you’re attending a cold-weather celebration.
