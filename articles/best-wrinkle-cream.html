I Tested the Wrinkle Creams Dermatologists Swear By

Find the best wrinkle cream for your antiaging concerns that dermatologists and aestheticians actually use. Shop brands like SkinCeuticals, RoC, and L’Oréal Paris.

By Jenny Berg

October 5, 2026

Wrinkles happen to the best of us—all of us, actually, if we have the privilege of aging. At 46, I’m in full defense mode. Of course, there are widely known techniques for keeping aging skin luminous, soft, and smooth, but those solutions are often found in a medical office. So as someone who’s naturally inclined to try topical treatments before anything else, I’ve wondered whether the best wrinkle creams actually work. Someone who would know? Shereene Idriss, MD, a board-certified dermatologist known for her no-nonsense, straight-talking skin-care advice.

“The effectiveness of wrinkle creams largely depends on their active ingredients,” Idriss says. “No cream is ever going to replace Botox and fillers, but wrinkle creams with the right active ingredients can help with the appearance of wrinkles over time.” And, good news: According to Robert Schwarcz, MD, a double-board-certified oculofacial plastic surgeon, creams are getting smarter all the time. “I used to tell patients that wrinkle creams offered only modest benefits,” he says. “And while that’s still true for deep lines and significant skin laxity, today’s formulations are more sophisticated. We’re seeing better delivery systems, more stable active ingredients, and formulas that combine retinoids, peptides, antioxidants, growth factors, and barrier-supporting ingredients that can help improve skin quality.”

This emphasis on improving overall skin quality—rather than on strict wrinkle repair—reflects a broader shift in skin care. “A number of my patients either ask me about or use K-beauty products as part of their skin-care routine,” says May Hall, DO, a double-board-certified dermatologist. “K-beauty really homes in on skin-barrier health and skin hydration as well as long-term preservation and skin support. As such, patients are seeking thoughtful, intentional skin-care products with hybrid formulations designed to build skin resilience and boost collagen, but also protect the skin barrier.”

Hall notes that creams with barrier-supporting ingredients like hyaluronic acid, ceramides, and squalane work well to lock in hydration and prevent transepidermal water loss, which can make wrinkles look more pronounced. Still, for those whose skin can tolerate active ingredients, doctors remain loyal to the hallowed gold standard of antiaging skin care: retinoids. The reason? It’s simple: “Vitamin-A derivatives, whether retinoid, retinol, or tretinoin, remain the mainstay of antiaging because they accelerate cell turnover and increase collagen production,” says board-certified plastic surgeon Carey Campbell, MD. “This is second only to SPF in my book, and every woman serious about antiaging needs one in her routine.”

And even if that routine includes in-office procedures, a good wrinkle cream should still be a mainstay. As Campbell puts it, “Some patients never had a routine before surgery, and the procedure becomes the catalyst that finally gets them started, which is great. But the patients who were already dedicated to skin care, had surgery with a resurfacing procedure alongside their facelift, and then stayed committed to their routine afterward are the ones who genuinely look like they’ve aged in reverse.”

That was enough for me to call in a slew of wrinkle cream samples, prioritizing the formulas that skin-care pros and Devon editors recommend. Whether or not I do end up putting my wrinkles in professional hands, these are the creams I’ll happily keep on my vanity.

Everything You Need to Know

Do antiaging wrinkle creams actually work?

Do antiaging creams turn back time? Obviously not, however much we wish, but a top-notch cream does much to keep the skin in its smoothest, plumpest, and firmest possible shape—supporting graceful aging rather than fighting the natural process as a whole. The key? Finding a cream with key ingredients that tackle your specific concern, whether it’s niacinamide for regulating oil production, vitamin C for brightening, or retinol for, well, nearly anyone who wants to kick-start cellular renewal. Consistency also counts, as not even the best antiaging creams work overnight. And a little help from supporting players goes a long way. Pairing your antiaging cream with a well-formulated sunscreen for the face is key to protecting collagen and elastin from more rapid degradation.

A Dermatologist’s Tip for Picking an Anti-Aging Cream

“Choose your cream based on your skin type and goals rather than marketing claims,” says Kristina Collins, MD, a board-certified dermatologist and Mohs micrographic surgeon in Austin, Texas. She breaks down her top tips below:

  • “For dry skin, opt for a rich moisturizer with peptides and ceramides.”
  • “ Acne-prone skin does better with lighter formulas that include retinol or bakuchiol.”
  • For sensitive skin, “fragrance-free and dermatologist-tested products are always best to minimize irritation.”

What Antiaging Ingredients to Look For

“Retinoids remain the gold standard because they boost collagen and increase cell turnover,” says Collins. “Peptides, niacinamide, and antioxidants such as vitamin C improve firmness and tone, while hyaluronic acid and ceramides support hydration and barrier function.” Here’s a deeper dive into the ingredients that make antiaging creams stand out, according to the pros:

  • Vitamin A: Hartman echoes Collins’s claim that retinoids, which are vitamin-A derivatives, are MVPs in antiaging creams. “Retinoids’ benefits are numerous and unparalleled,” he says. “Using one is arguably the most important step that you can take for overall skin health and antiaging after sun protection factor.”
  • Exfoliating Agents: Penzi stresses the importance of exfoliation. “Low-dose AHAs, like glycolic acid, act as a gentle exfoliator to smooth out the appearance of wrinkles,” she says. Marino notes: “Overall, when you exfoliate and hydrate the skin, you will see less texture, more radiance, and more bounce to the skin, making it look more youthful.”
  • SPF: Want the real , number one antiaging trick? You’ve likely heard it before, but it bears repeating that consistent use of sunscreen with an SPF of at least 30 is the best thing you can do to preserve collagen, elastin, and an overall youthful glow. “It doesn’t matter what wrinkle cream you’re applying on your face at night,” aesthetician Taylor Worden says. “If you aren’t applying SPF during the day to protect your skin, then you are wasting your time and money.”
  • Growth Factors: Marisa Garshick, MD, a board-certified dermatologist in New York and New Jersey, explains the appeal behind the buzzy ingredient. “Growth factors serve as signaling molecules to stimulate the production of collagen, which in turn improves the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles,” she says. As a bonus, growth factors are typically well tolerated by all skin types.
  • Peptides: Garshick also recommends peptides for those looking to diminish the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles. By stimulating collagen production, the short chains of amino acids can effectively tighten the skin. What’s more, peptides such as argireline or matrixyl can also help improve the appearance of expression lines by blocking the release of neurotransmitters. “Peptides can be used by all skin types but may be especially good for those who cannot tolerate retinol and those with sensitive skin,” Garshick notes.
  • Vitamin C: Dermatologists are quick to agree that this brightening antioxidant has its place in nearly any antiaging routine. By protecting the skin from free-radical damage, the antiaging superhero can protect the skin from collagen loss and contribute to a firmer, more youthful skin look.
  • Hyaluronic Acid: “This is a super hydrator that won’t weigh your skin down,” says dermatologist Deanna Mraz, MD. “It’s excellent for helping the skin regulate hydration levels, which improves the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles.”

When should you start using an antiaging cream?

“Prevention should start in your mid-20s, when collagen production naturally begins to slow down,” says Collins. In addition to an antiaging cream, she recommends that those in their mid-20s consider using a gentle retinol or antioxidant serum. “These can be introduced early on to build healthy habits and maintain long-term skin health and quality,” she says.

When to Apply Antiaging Cream in Your Skin-Care Routine

According to Collins, the best rule of thumb is to apply antiaging creams ​​after cleansing and serums. “Use it before sunscreen in the morning,” she says. “At night, antiaging creams are usually the last step after treatment serums and before heavier moisturizers, if needed.”

What do dermatologists recommend to treat wrinkles?

If wrinkles are your primary concern, consider following dermatologist Dendy Engelman, MD’s advice. “I typically recommend a combination of daily sun protection and a retinoid to help boost collagen and improve the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles,” she says. She adds that hydrating ingredients such as hyaluronic acid help plump and smooth out the skin, which softens fine lines. For more pronounced wrinkles, Engelman recommends adding in-office procedures to an at-home regimen.Collins concurs: “A combination approach works best for treating wrinkles,” she says. “Use topical retinoids and peptides for daily maintenance, and incorporate in-office treatments such as neuromodulators, laser resurfacing, or microneedling for deeper wrinkles.” And of course, she adds: “Sun protection every day is essential.”

Do antiaging creams work as well as in-office treatments?

“Topical products can improve texture and brightness, but in-office procedures penetrate deeper and produce more dramatic, faster results,” Collins says. “Daily use of creams helps preserve and extend those professional results over time.”Considering a professional treatment? Here are a few to keep in mind, broken down by Idriss:

  • “Botox effectively relaxes muscles to diminish dynamic wrinkles, while dermal fillers restore volume to deeper lines.”
  • “Resurfacing lasers [such as CO2 and IPL] exfoliate the skin and stimulate collagen production, enhancing texture over time.”
  • “Microneedling creates controlled injuries that trigger collagen production.”

Do antiaging creams help prevent wrinkles?

“Unfortunately, nothing can completely prevent wrinkles,” says Rayva Khanna, MD, a dermatology resident at Georgetown University Medical Center. “However, wrinkle creams often contain active ingredients that stimulate collagen production and help slow the development of fine lines.” Her top tip for choosing a cream to tackle any sign of aging? This won’t be the first time you’ve heard this today: “Look for an antiaging cream with a retinoid,” she says. “Retinoids, derived from vitamin A, promote skin turnover, boost collagen synthesis, and improve pigmentary changes. They’re among the most well-studied ingredients for antiaging.”

What causes wrinkles?

Mraz explains that there are two distinct types of wrinkles: static and dynamic. “Static wrinkles are the ones that appear even when our face is at rest, while dynamic wrinkles appear while the face is moving,” she says. “For example, smile lines by the eyes when you’re grinning big.” The natural loss of collagen and elastin over time also causes wrinkles. Genetics do come into play, as well as lifestyle factors such as UV exposure, smoking, diet, and skin care.

How Often to Use an Antiaging Cream

“Most anti-aging creams can be used once or twice daily, depending on skin tolerance and formulation,” Collins says. As a general rule, “Retinoids may need to be introduced gradually, starting every other night.” Malika Sloan, medical aesthetician and founder of Sloan Studio in New York City, says that her philosophy is to “keep it simple,” using an anti-aging cream as though it’s a regular moisturizer (provided, of course, that its ingredients are safe to use day and night, and suit your skin type.) “Most of the time, your wrinkle cream can replace your regular moisturizer rather than becoming another step you have to add to your routine,” she notes. “Apply it after your serums and treatments, and in the morning always finish with SPF.”

What to Look for in an Antiaging Cream

“Look for proven actives like retinol, peptides, antioxidants, and SPF—and avoid heavy fragrance or drying alcohols that can cause irritation,” Collins says. She adds that dermatologist-developed or clinically tested formulas are generally the most reliable and effective picks. According to Tanya Kormeili, MD, a board-certified dermatologist in Santa Monica, California, “Using a moisturizer that helps restore the barrier and keeps the skin protected is a very clever way to keep the skin healthy in winter. While choosing an antiaging skin cream, stay away from harsh acids and agents that may further dry the skin.” She leans toward products that have ceramides, hyaluronic acid, and squalene as their base and contain peptides, growth factors, and vitamins as their active ingredients.

Is it necessary to use a separate eye cream in addition to a wrinkle cream?

“This can go either way depending on your specific concerns and, honestly, how lengthy of a routine you’re willing to have,” says Sloan. “For my lower-maintenance clients who have general aging concerns and simply want to maintain healthy, hydrated skin, a separate eye cream isn’t always necessary.” In many cases, she says, a facial wrinkle cream can be brought up around the eye area, as long as the formula is gentle and doesn’t contain active ingredients, such as retinol, that may be irritating. However, “If you have a more specific concern—dark circles, dullness, puffiness, fine lines or that crepey texture around the eyes—that’s where I think a targeted eye product can really make sense,” Sloan adds. “The skin around our eyes is thinner and more delicate, and there are some really beautiful formulas designed specifically for that area with ingredients targeted to those individual concerns.” In her personal routine, Sloan likes using a thinner eye product with a gel or serum consistency. Then, she brings her face cream around the eye area for additional hydration.
