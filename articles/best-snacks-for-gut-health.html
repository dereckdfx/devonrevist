The 9 Best Snack Options to Support Your Gut Health

Snacks can actually help boost your gut health: experts break down the best food options for day’s smallest meal that won’t cause bloating or indigestion.

By Audrey Noble

September 11, 2026

Who among us hasn’t reached for the nearest salty or sweet snack in a moment of crisis (a.k.a. the afternoon slump)? And while it’s fine for a quick pick-me-up, that treat may not be the best for your long-term gut health. Instead of always reaching for a bag of salty chips or sour gummies, you might want to consider some options that feed your microbiome as much as your afternoon fancies. As it turns out, even the smallest meal can do wonders—or impact—for your digestion.

As sports dietitian Roxana Ehsani, MS, RD, explains, the right snack foods can feed your gut bacteria healthy fibers so it can flourish and grow, and they can also help prevent constipation and support your digestive system overall. Real Nutrition founder Amy Shapiro, RD, agrees and adds that healthy snacks can help prevent those midafternoon energy crashes and set you up for the rest of the day. “The goal is supporting steady energy and gut function, not constant grazing,” Shapiro says.

Ingredients to look for (and avoid)

Both Ehsani and Shapiro recommend looking for a snack that combines protein and fiber to best support your gut. Ehsani explains that it will help steady your blood sugar and satiety, while Shapiro adds that it will promote your gut microbiome.

Best snacks for gut health

Some of the best snacks for a healthy gut include:

Yogurt is one of the most popular and easiest probiotic foods that you can incorporate into any meals. As a snack, Ehsani says, every spoonful provides you with gut-friendly bacteria to support a more balanced and diverse gut microbiome. Shapiro agrees and recommends pairing it with fruits like apple slices, berries, and pears for added prebiotic fiber content.

Shapiro recommends pairing veggies with a nutritious dip. Something like a hummus and edamame combo gives you a plant protein option loaded with fiber, she says.

Even in small doses, chia pudding packs a nutritional punch. Shapiro says it is a rich source of fiber and omega-3s to help support your gut. Try it with honey, fruit, and nuts for an afternoon pick-me-up.

Shapiro says that roasted chickpeas make for a healthy alternative to processed chips. On top of being tasty and filling, they are also rich in fiber.

Fermented veggies are an easy way to add probiotics between meals. Shapiro recommends pairing a fermented vegetable like pickles with cheese or lean meats like turkey slices for added protein.

Pistachios are great for your gut. Ehsani points to a study that shows people with prediabetes experienced a positive boost in their gut bacteria after eating pistachios every night for 12 weeks. They are also a source of fiber to help prevent constipation. “They are easy, fun, and quick to enjoy for a snack,” she says. “You can toss them onto a yogurt, a piece of toast, or whirl into a smoothie.”

Prunes get a bad rap for being outdated, but they are packed with nutrients and great for gut health. Ehsani says that prunes contain insoluble and soluble fiber, which can support the bacteria in your gut, and contain natural sugar alcohol sorbitol, which can help with bowel movements. She recommends pairing prunes with walnuts, which she says are rich in omega-3 fatty acids and fiber. Studies even show that walnuts can also support your gut bacteria.

If you’re looking for a sweet snack, try dates. Dates contain fiber, and studies show that it can help with frequent bowel movements. Dip them in dark chocolate and sprinkle on some sea salt for added flavor.

Ehsani says that the fermented milk contains probiotic cultures that can support a more balanced and diverse gut microbiome and regular digestion. Consider making a kefir smoothie for a more filling snack.

Other things to consider

Shapiro recommends avoiding ultraprocessed snack foods with added sugars, artificial sweeteners, and emulsifiers, as those will increase risk of disrupting your gut microbiome. As with any meal, it’s always best to opt for whole-food options. “Snacks are a great time to fill in the blanks in your day when it comes to nutrition,” Ehsani agrees.

Timing is also key. Shapiro says to give your gut three to four hours between eating to support your digestive process. So healthy snacking can be just as tasty as some of our favorite guilty pleasures. Something to think about for the sake of your gut.
